**Why the width number is trustworthy even though the distances may not be:**
width is measured at a FIXED position from the balls shot there, so it does not
depend on the pose at all. The distances could be off (a rear wheel was dead,
the robot was partly pushed, and `cam trim` was never checked) and "the band is
wide" would still hold. That is why the conclusion rests on width, not slope.

## The two leads worth chasing

**1. Extend the RANGE, don't refine inside it.** 20–52 in is close. The camera
reads tags at 30–60 in and matches are shot from further out. If a distance term
exists it will show up between 55 and 80 in, not between 20 and 52. Next session:
3–4 spots at 55 / 65 / 75 in, turret near 0 (back to the hive).

**2. Extreme turret angles may genuinely shoot worse.** The −96.6° spot scored
**43%**, far the worst in the set, and the two extremes differ by 88 RPM
(+92° → 1305, −96.6° → 1217) which is the shape of a `b·sin(turret)` term with
b ≈ 44. But those two spots are at 20 in and 45 in, so angle and distance are
confounded and it is two points. **To separate them: 3–4 spots at the SAME
distance with the turret at −90 / −45 / 0 / +45 / +90.** That is the phase-2
sweep, and it is now the higher-value half.

## CORRECTION, same evening: the extreme-angle spots are MECHANICAL, not ballistic

Shane, after reviewing the session:

> "When the collector was pointing toward the red alliance and shooting at the
> red hive a lot of those shots were hitting the camera or cord on the front of
> the turret. I think that's because of the placement of the flywheels and the
> center of the flywheels is closer to the deflector so it was hitting the
> deflector lower and coming out lower. It might have even been hitting a ridge
> at the very bottom of the deflector. I think the lower RPM simply wasn't
> hitting the bad spot or wasn't deflecting as much into the camera."

This is the right explanation and it is worth understanding, because the layout
makes it inevitable rather than accidental:

- the flywheels are bolted to the CHASSIS and fire up through the turret hole;
- the deflector rotates WITH the turret;
- so the point where the ball meets the deflector moves as the turret turns, and
  at large turret angles it lands low on the deflector -- possibly on a ridge at
  the bottom -- and exits lower than designed;
- and the camera sits `CAMERA_FORWARD = 4.5` in **in front of the turret axis,
  pointing the way the turret points**, which is exactly where the ball exits.
  The ball clears the camera only if it leaves at the designed height.

### LEAD 2 IS RETRACTED. Do not fit the sin term.

The earlier note called the 88 RPM difference between +92 deg and -96.6 deg the
shape of a `b*sin(turret)` term with b ~ 44, and called the angle sweep the
higher-value half. **That is wrong and acting on it would be harmful.** The 1217
at -96.6 deg is not "the RPM that scores from there" -- it is the RPM that
happened to miss the obstruction. Fitting it would bake a mechanical defect into
the shot model and then faithfully reproduce it, which is calibrating around a
fault instead of fixing it.

A real flywheel-offset term may still exist. It cannot be measured until the
interference is gone.

### HAZARD: the camera is the aiming sensor, and balls have been hitting it

`CAMERA_PITCH_DEG = 38` was measured with an inclinometer earlier the same day,
and the whole aiming chain now rests on it. A ball striking the camera or its
cord can move the mount, and nothing in software would notice -- the aim would
just quietly go wrong.

**Cheap check before trusting aiming again: run Tag Pose Test and read `lvl`.**
It was +0.8..+1.3 deg after the pitch fix. The turret base is physically level,
so `lvl` is a direct measurement of how wrong the declared pitch is. If it has
moved off ~1 deg, the camera moved.

### WHAT TO DO, in order

1. **Fix the interference mechanically.** Options, cheapest first: trim or
   relieve the ridge at the bottom of the deflector; raise or shield the camera
   out of the ball path; shorten/reroute the cord. Shielding the camera also
   protects the calibration.
2. **Then measure the SAFE SHOOTING ARC.** Sweep the turret in steps with the
   flywheels running and find the angle where the ball stops leaving cleanly.
   That arc is almost certainly NARROWER than the turret's +/-150 of travel, and
   the difference is a real constraint on aiming and on auto routes -- a turret
   that can POINT somewhere it cannot SHOOT from.
3. **Only then** re-run the angle sweep at one distance for the offset fit.
4. Worth considering once the arc is known: a software guard that warns (or
   refuses) when the aim solution falls outside the shooting arc, separate from
   `MIN_ANGLE`/`MAX_ANGLE`. Not worth building before the arc is measured.
