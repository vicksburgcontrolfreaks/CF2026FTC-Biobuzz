## START HERE: the next thing is a wrench, not a keyboard

**Balls are hitting the camera at large turret angles, and the camera is the
aiming sensor.** Shane spotted it reviewing the session: with the collector
pointed at the red alliance and shooting the red hive, a lot of shots struck the
camera or its cord on the front of the turret.

Why the layout makes it inevitable rather than unlucky: the flywheels are bolted
to the **chassis** and fire up through the turret hole, the deflector rotates
**with** the turret, so the point where the ball meets the deflector moves as
the turret turns. At large angles it lands low on the deflector — possibly on a
ridge at the bottom — and exits low, straight into a camera that sits
`CAMERA_FORWARD = 4.5` in ahead of the turret axis pointing wherever the turret
points.

**The order of work, and nothing past step 1 is worth starting first:**

1. **Fix the interference mechanically.** Cheapest first: trim or relieve the
   ridge at the bottom of the deflector; raise or shield the camera out of the
   ball path; shorten or reroute the cord. Shielding the camera also protects
   the calibration.
2. **Then measure the SAFE SHOOTING ARC.** Sweep the turret in steps with the
   flywheels running and find where the ball stops leaving cleanly. That arc is
   almost certainly narrower than the turret's ±150° of travel — a turret that
   can **point** where it cannot **shoot** — and the gap is a real constraint on
   aiming and on auto routes.
3. **Only then** re-run the angle sweep at one distance for the offset fit.
4. Worth considering once the arc is known: a software guard that warns or
   refuses when the aim solution falls outside the shooting arc, separate from
   `MIN_ANGLE` / `MAX_ANGLE`. Not worth building before the arc is measured.

### Before trusting aiming again, 30 seconds of robot time

`CAMERA_PITCH_DEG = 38` was measured with an inclinometer hours before the balls
started hitting the mount, and the whole aiming chain rests on it. **Run Tag
Pose Test and read the `lvl` line.** The turret base is physically level, so
`lvl` measures declared-vs-actual pitch directly. It read **+0.8 to +1.3°** after
the pitch fix. If it has moved off ~1°, a ball moved the camera and the aim is
quietly wrong — nothing in software would notice.

## The headline result: one RPM covers 20–52 in. Don't build a table.

7 CSVs pulled off the Control Hub over wifi — 263 shots / 8 spots in the big
session, 103 / 3 in the earlier one. Raw data in `docs/shotmap-data/`, full
write-up in `docs/shot-map-2026-11-13.md`.

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| RPM vs distance, all 8 spots | **−0.47 RPM/in** (wrong sign, −15 RPM across the span) |
| RPM vs distance, near-zero turret only (6 spots) | **+0.78 RPM/in** (correct sign, +19 RPM) |
| mean band width | **~101 RPM** |
| spread of balance points | 108 RPM |

The band is as wide as the entire variation, so the variation is noise. **One RPM
near 1287 sits inside every band measured.** `ShotTable` stays empty on purpose —
that is the answer, not a gap. Set one RPM and move on.

**Why this survives suspect distances:** band width is measured at a fixed
position from the balls shot there, so it does not depend on the pose. A rear
wheel was dead, the robot was partly pushed, and `cam trim` was never checked —
the distances may be off, and "the band is wide" still holds. The conclusion
rests on **width**, not slope.

It also explains what Shane noticed while shooting — "several shots that were
makes, but barely, or would have shot well over." The band really is that
forgiving; he was reading the mechanism correctly.

### A retraction, written down so nobody acts on it

The first analysis called the 88 RPM gap between +92° and −96.6° the shape of a
`b·sin(turret)` term with b ≈ 44, and called the angle sweep the higher-value
half of phase 2. **Retracted.** The 1217 at −96.6° is not the RPM that scores
from there, it is the RPM that happened to miss the obstruction. Fitting it would
bake a mechanical defect into the shot model and then faithfully reproduce it.
A real flywheel-offset term may exist; it cannot be measured until the
interference is gone.

Dropping the two contaminated spots is what makes the distance term come out the
way physics requires. An obstruction produces a backwards slope; ballistics does
not. That is the part that convinces.

## What else changed last night

### The turret now faces rearward — and this is NOT robot-verified

No hardware change: the magnet rides on a turret bracket under the camera, so
the camera-to-magnet angle never changes, and with no hard stops which side of
the magnet the turret works on is a software choice.

| constant | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| `RobotProfile` 6494 `TURRET_ZERO_DEG` | 0 | **180** |
| `Turret` 6494 `HOME_SWITCH_ANGLE` | −85.5 | **79.5** |
| `MAGNET_BAND_DEG` | 10.1 | **9.1** |
| `MIN`/`MAX_ANGLE` | −90 / 90 | **−150 / 150** |
| `HOMING_DIRECTION` | −1 | **+1** (CCW, toward the magnet) |
| `HOMING_SEARCH_MAX` | 150 | **110** |

The win: the blind arc goes from 180° to 60°. Two protections were weakened on
purpose, both documented at the constants:

- `MIN_ANGLE` used to sit inside the magnet band so a homed turret could not park
  past the magnet. At ±150 it can, and homing then fails by design. **The
  pre-home instruction is now the only guard, and it inverted — "roughly
  REARWARD."**
- The homing search used to run toward the cable's slack side; it now runs toward
  the tight side. Hence 110: enough to find the magnet from centre, not enough to
  wind the cable up.

**Step 1 on the robot is checking the cable by hand across the new window before
anything is powered.** The laptop suite builds its own made-up robots in
`TurretGeometryTest` / `OdometryAimTest` / `LaunchReachTest` on purpose, so it
never reads these constants: green means the math is unchanged, **not** that the
numbers are right. If homing parks ~9° off, the edge pair is swapped — use 88.6
with `HOMING_DIRECTION −1`; the comment says so.

The shot-map session was shot after the flip and reached ±96°, so travel itself
clearly works. Homing direction and the park position are the parts to confirm.

### Direct feed: hold the button, the indexer runs

`Launcher.DIRECT_FEED`, **default true.** Shane: "our current state is really
more of a do what we say kind of mode where we hold a button and it does exactly
that for as long as we want it to." The old path had **five** ways to stop the
indexer while the button was still held — `NO_BALL_SEC`, the `RECOVERING`
re-wait, one-ball-per-queue-slot, `DIP_RPM` doubling as the shot counter, and
`NectarGuard` latching FAULT. Four of those are last year's flipper sequence
translated onto the indexer motor and never validated against this mechanism.

Driver-feel calls: the flywheel stops on release rather than latching, and the
first ball waits for the wheels **once** per hold. Every press therefore pays the
spin-up, so **Y is repurposed from on/off latch to PRE-SPIN** — hold the wheels
up between volleys and the next press feeds immediately. Backward (LT) still wins
over fire.

**It reverts in one line at the event.** `ShooterLogicTest` (22) and
`NectarGuardTest` (24) still encode the old behaviour and still pass; neither
class was touched. Auto was checked, not assumed: `DIRECT_FEED` forces
`LAUNCH_FULL_TIME` and reports "shots not counted (direct feed)" instead of a
misleading "TIMED OUT at 0 shots".

### The trim cap came off: `MAX_TRIM_DEG` 15 → 45

Shane, during a driver session, on the pose only being re-trusted when the camera
agrees: "we're likely to keep driving around the field for the entire match
waiting for the camera to agree to the pose... This logic makes little sense
unless I'm misunderstanding you." He wasn't.

1. **The trim IS the correction.** A pose 20° out gives a 20° trim, and applying
   it makes the aim right. Refusing it just discards the measurement.
2. **Giving up applies the same correction anyway** — the camera fallback points
   at `turretAngle + cameraBearing`, the full trim by another route. They only
   differ once tags leave view, where odometry carries the learned correction and
   camera aiming has nothing. So the cap bought nothing.
3. The wrong-cell guard it was sized for is already done by `isTargetTag`.
4. Nothing was ever going to fix the pose — `TagLocalizer` is wired into nothing.

`TRIM_SMOOTHING` 0.15 means even a frame at the cap moves the aim under 7°, and
`distrustPose()` still fires on sustained disagreement past 45 — that's a fault,
not drift. **Honest limit, also in the code:** the trim corrects the aim, not the
pose, so a stale trim carried while blind with a *position* error slowly goes
wrong as the robot moves, and re-converges when tags return.

**Side effect to know about:** "NOT TRUSTED" no longer trips at 15°, so the
warning the Shot Map header used to tell people to watch for mostly will not
appear. The header now says to watch **`cam trim`** instead, and says why.

### The wall re-anchor is wired — gamepad 1 D-pad Down, and LT + BACK in Shot Map

A drifted pose does **not** heal itself: once it has wandered past
`MAX_TRIM_DEG`, the turret stays on camera aiming indefinitely however many tags
it sees. Correcting the pose is the only road back, and once corrected odometry
resumes on its own one agreeing frame later, with no re-init.

- **`DriveTeleOp`: gamepad 1 D-pad Down** (read off gamepad1 directly so it works
  in solo). Face is FRONT, collector-first — back-first would point the camera at
  the wall, which is what the flip was for. Long rumble = accepted, two blips =
  refused, and the reason stays on screen.
- **Shot Map: LT + BACK.** Plain BACK still wins when you're actually on the
  start spot: that pose is known, the re-anchor's is measured.
- On accept: `follower.setPose()`, then `aim.resetPhase()` — the trim was measured
  against the pose just replaced, so keeping it would aim where the hive *was*.
- The guard layer is `WallFix` + 21 tests, first-failure-wins:
  no fix → still moving → turret unsettled → off the field → in a flower → tags
  disagree → sight line too oblique → heading snap too big → ACCEPT. Motion
  before geometry (stop first, the geometry complaint may evaporate), and
  physically-impossible before merely-noisy.
- **Its one unverified assumption is that `ftcPose` on `TurretCamera` is
  camera-relative.** Shot Map is the cheap place to find that out — a bad answer
  costs one CSV row instead of a match.

Also fixed: the stale "about to drop to camera aiming" warning that never
dropped. `rejecting` latched when tags left view — normal while driving — so the
screen said it forever while nothing was being measured. The no-usable-bearing
path clears the flag now; the drop *behaviour* is unchanged, only the reporting.

### Why the camera was reading 1–2 tags, and the 9% that explained three symptoms

Shane caught this by noticing two numbers disagreed: **TAGS read 4 while QUALITY
read 1–2.** Those are different questions — TAGS is what the decoder got off the
image, QUALITY's count is `lastFix.tagsUsed`, what survived the localization
chain. Being told to read QUALITY for the tag count pointed two sessions of work
at the camera. **The camera was fine.** `TagLocalizer.solve()` was dropping good
tags for `floorError > FLOOR_TOLERANCE` (4 in) with nothing on screen saying so.

Tag Pose Test now shows the funnel — `decoded 4 → posed 4 → believable 1 →
used 1` — plus a per-tag line with id, bearing, z and which test failed
(`FLOOR` / `LVL` / `(nopose)` / `(nofix)`).

Then the cause: **the C270 focal length is overridden 822.317 → 902**, from Shane
measuring with a tape instead of inferring. Lens to the centre of the raised red
audience cell from ~(57, 9): **62 in actual, Tag Pose Test reporting 56–57.**
Reported range is proportional to focal length, so f_true = 822.317 × 62/56.5 ≈
902. That single 9% accounts for three things chased separately for two sessions:

- the turret base solving **z = +3.1…+4.0** while sitting on the floor
  (0.09 × 35.2 in of height = 3.2, independent of distance, which is why z looked
  like a fixed offset);
- the **~7 in position error at ~60 in** (0.09 × 60 = 5.6);
- the **tag count collapsing to 1–2** — `FLOOR_TOLERANCE` is 4 and z sits at 3.5,
  so good tags straddle the limit.

**This is not a real calibration.** It is the SDK's C270 entry with the focal
length scaled by one measurement at one distance near frame centre, and it leaves
the distortion coefficients alone — so it does nothing for the ~4× frame-edge
error. A checkerboard calibration should replace it. Validation order is in the
file comment: range vs tape at **two** distances, then z → 0, then four
believable tags, then the position error.

Also retracted in the process: **"6 ms is too dark" was wrong.** 6 ms / gain 250
decodes 4 tags and degrades *upward*, gone by ~14 ms. `Mode.Auto` in the SDK is
single-trigger, not continuous — and "set it to auto" is not "never set it,"
which is the state the working config was actually in.

### Shot Map usability, from running a session solo

- **LT + Y latches the collector.** RT ran collector and indexer together, so
  holding it to keep feeding also fired. Split: indexer on RT, collector latched
  on LT + Y. Balls stack at the indexer and RT sends exactly one, which is the
  one-ball-per-press the ladder needs. On screen as its own line, because a
  latched motor running with nobody touching anything should never be a surprise.
- **The scoring convention for lucky makes is now in the header.** When the make
  and the trajectory disagree, **press the trajectory** — sailing high is LONG
  even if it went in. A rung scores (highs − lows) / n, so HIT counts as zero:
  scoring lucky makes as HIT softens the crossing rather than biasing it. Cost:
  "best hit rate" then means clean hits, which reads lower than the match score.
- **The log path is `/sdcard/shotmap/`, not `/sdcard/FIRST/shotmap/`** —
  `AppUtil.ROOT_FOLDER` is `/sdcard`. It lives on the **Control Hub**, not the
  Driver Station, Android does not expose `/sdcard` as mass storage, and `adb`
  over wifi (`adb connect 192.168.43.1:5555`) needs no cable at all.
